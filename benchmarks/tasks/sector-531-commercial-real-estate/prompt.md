# Did the office building's operating changes save 35 percent?

Plant A here is a multi-tenant office building of more than 100,000 square feet. Its owner tuned the building automation, added sensors, put variable frequency drives on fans and pumps, and upgraded heat pumps, ventilation and lighting, mostly at no or low cost.

A US Department of Energy Better Buildings showcase of a project like this (<https://betterbuildingssolutioncenter.energy.gov/showcase-projects/nuveen-real-estate-no-and-low-cost-energy-savings-1001-pennsylvania-avenue>) reports a 35% energy saving. The building's FoaMES site reports occupant-hours from badge access as its output and the electricity meter. The asset manager wants FoaMES's reduction in energy per occupant-hour next to the claim, since occupancy changed between the two weeks. A building-wide alarm that clears the floors is unplanned downtime; there is no scrap counter, so the `scrap` counter stays at zero.

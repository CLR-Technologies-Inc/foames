# Did the press redesign cut the compressed air energy as reported?

Plant A prints personal and business checks on 70 machines, two shifts a day. As new, faster machines arrived, the site's air demand more than doubled, to over 600 scfm at 130 psig, and the plant faced buying more compressor capacity. Instead, the machine supplier replaced the machines' air bars with small onboard blowers, the plant fixed leaks, added storage and lowered the header pressure.

A US Department of Energy case study of a project like this (DOE/GO-102002-1546, <https://docs.nrel.gov/docs/fy02osti/31713.pdf>, a US government work) reports demand falling to about 300 scfm at 81 psig and gives 2.9 million kWh a year in its summary. Plant A's FoaMES site meters the compressor room. The facilities manager wants the measured saving per thousand pages printed set against that figure.

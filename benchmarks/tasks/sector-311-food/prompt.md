# Did the compressed air renovation save what the case study says?

Plant A spray-dries and packs infant formula and nutritional powders. Its compressed air plant fed the dryers, the fluid beds and the filling lines through a header that ran 95 to 105 psig, and about a third of the peak air flow was leakage. The plant repaired leaks, added storage and held the header at a lower, steady pressure.

A US Department of Energy case study of a project like this (DOE/GO-102001-1330, <https://nrel.gov/docs/fy01osti/30231.pdf>, a US government work) reports a saving of 2,542,000 kWh a year. Plant A's FoaMES site recorded a week before the change and a week after it. The energy manager wants to know what FoaMES's own records say about that figure, and wants a write-up the plant could later publish once measured data replaces the illustrative events.

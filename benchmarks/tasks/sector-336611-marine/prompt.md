# Does the shipyard's meter show its lighting and compressed air saving?

Plant A builds ships, about ten a year, working six days a week. Its fabrication halls were lit by old high-bay lamps that ran all day, and its compressors ran without coordinated controls. A university Industrial Assessment Center funded by the US Department of Energy assessed a yard like it, and the yard carried out all four recommendations: LED lamps, daylighting, compressor controls and timers.

The assessment's record (IAC assessment LS2503, <https://itac.university/assessment/LS2503>, from the DOE Industrial Assessment Center database) gives the saving as 2,873,935 kWh a year, close to 30% of the yard's electricity. A ship takes months to build, so Plant A's FoaMES site reports steel tonnes through the panel line and block assembly as its output (rework is `scrap`) and meters the yard. The yard's engineering manager wants the measured saving per tonne set against the claim.

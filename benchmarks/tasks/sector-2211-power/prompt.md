# Does the generating unit burn less fuel per MWh after the coal dryer?

Unit A burns lignite that arrives at about 40 percent moisture, so part of its fuel's heat goes into boiling off water. The station added fluidised-bed dryers heated by the plant's own waste heat, so the boiler gets drier coal.

A US Department of Energy announcement about a project like this (<https://www.energy.gov/node/605426>, a US government work) reports that the station's overall performance improved by almost 4 percent. It does not report fuel per MWh, so the claim checked here is the assumed equivalent: read as a 4 percent heat-rate gain, that is 1 − 1/1.04 = 3.85 percent less fuel heat per MWh. State that basis in `CASE_STUDY.md`. Unit A's FoaMES site records net generation as the line's output and the fuel heat input as the meter. The performance engineer wants FoaMES's measured change in fuel heat per MWh next to that figure. Load-following hours count as Idle; there is no scrap counter, so the `scrap` counter stays at zero.
